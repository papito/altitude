Colors

Every color token of :root in core.css, each theme and semantic color a literal light-dark(light, dark) pair of hex values. The Theme colors are the brand colors (--theme-primary, the one hue of the chrome, with its text and on-color; --theme-secondary, the supporting surface; --theme-accent, the call-to-action color, with its on-color), the neutral scale (--theme-neutral-1 to -4, from the lightest surface to the darkest text in the light theme: the field, the page, lines and secondary text, text) and --theme-on-field, the text typed into a field. A rule writes a theme color or a semantic color directly. The Derived cards are the veils, a theme color or --shadow-color at an alpha: the only other color tokens. A new color is a new theme role as a hex pair, or a new veil when it must be translucent. The four semantic colors (success, warning, error, info) and --shadow-color stand apart, with pairs of their own; a semantic fill is one HSL recipe over its color per scheme, shown with its From line. The setup page's palette is that page's own, in pages/setup.css, and is not scanned. A card with a recipe shows it and the tokens it comes from; Off palette marks a token that writes a color literal, and the literals written past the tokens are listed under Token audit. The app ships dark; the Light / Dark switch above the contents shows this page in either theme, both real, and is remembered in this browser. Click a token to copy its var() reference. The slider of --scrim-color repaints this page with the token's color of the theme shown at another alpha, until the page is reloaded.

Typography

One font stack, --font, and a scale of sizes in rem, --font-size-xs to --font-size-display, with --font-size-base on body: a font size is a step of the scale, never a literal. A weight is --font-weight-bold or --font-weight-medium, spaced-out text takes --letter-spacing-wide and text that wraps --line-height-text. A glyph's own size and leading (the spinner, the merge arrow, a category's badge) stay in its rule. The Sizes row shows the steps on the real components. The tester previews any font this browser has installed.

Spacing & sizing

Gaps (paddings, margins, gaps and insets) use a step of the --space-* scale, or a role token over it. Use the section space between large areas, such as panels and page paddings, and the control spaces between individual elements and list rows. A role card names the step it takes; the sizes are the lengths that are no gap, such as widths and heights. The scale and the sizes are in rem, so they follow the browser's default font size.

Borders & shadows

A border token carries width, style and color together; use it whole rather than restating its parts. --panel-border is the edge of a chrome panel and the divider inside one; --border-thin and --border-thick the edges of a cell, a menu or a quiet control, and of an input, a button or a dialog. A rectangular box or control rounds its corners with --radius, a badge with --radius-small; a circle or an avatar writes its percentage.

Panels & layout

A panel is edged with --panel-border, lit by --box-shadow and padded by --panel-padding; its text carries --text-shadow. Lay pages out with CSS Grid and Flexbox.

Buttons

Success, warning and error buttons signal an outcome, not emphasis. Hover and keyboard focus share one look, through :focus-visible and not :focus, so a button a dialog returns focus to is not left glowing after a mouse click. A choice between ways of showing the same thing is a .toggle-group: its buttons side by side, the current one aria-pressed="true". The theme switch above the contents is a live one.

Menus & dialogs

Both controls are live. A row's actions sit in a ⋯ context menu, which stays a list of actions; each action, like an Add button, opens a separate modal anchored below its control. Submit the dialog blank to see the validation state, or with a name to see a completed operation.
